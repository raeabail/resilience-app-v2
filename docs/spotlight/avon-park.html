Case Study 2: Avon Park Air Force Range Sentinel Landscape

Executive summary:

This case study applies the Resilience Scorecard framework across the Avon Park Air Force Range Sentinel Landscape (APAFRSL) and surrounding Central Florida Regional Planning Council (CFRPC) service area, which includes five counties in Florida's Heartland region. The assessment evaluates landscape resilience to wildfire, flooding, and extreme weather by integrating natural infrastructure, landscape condition, hydrologic function, and hazard exposure into a parcel-scale decision-support framework.

Results highlight areas where wetlands, lakes, recharge systems, fire-adapted ecosystems, and other natural landscape features contribute to long-term resilience while also identifying locations where development pressure, infrastructure sensitivity, or environmental vulnerability may increase risk. The framework is intended to support collaborative decision-making among military installations, conservation organizations, local governments, landowners, and regional planning partners.

A Landscape Built on Balance

The APAFRSL encompasses one of the most ecologically significant and operationally important regions in central Florida. The landscape supports military readiness, working lands, wildlife habitat, water resources, and rural communities while facing increasing challenges associated with extreme weather, flooding, wildfire, and long-term environmental change.

Unlike many regions where resilience is dominated by a single environmental challenge, resilience in central Florida is shaped by the interaction of fire, water, weather, and land management. Wetlands store floodwaters, recharge areas support groundwater systems, fire-adapted ecosystems reduce hazardous fuel accumulation, and natural landscapes help absorb and recover from recurring storm impacts. Together, these interconnected systems support military readiness, wildlife habitat, working lands, water resources, and rural communities.

Within this landscape, resilience is not simply the absence of risk. It is the capacity of natural and working lands to continue supporting military readiness, ecosystem function, and community stability despite ongoing environmental stressors.

What the Landscape Reveals

Rather than asking only where hazards occur, the resilience scorecard asks a broader question:

Where is the landscape most capable of withstanding disturbance and maintaining function over time?

Several consistent patterns emerged across the assessment.

  1. Water builds resilience. Areas associated with large wetland systems, connected lakes, groundwater recharge areas, and other hydrologically connected landscapes frequently exhibited higher resilience. These features store water, reduce runoff, support groundwater function, and moderate flood impacts during extreme precipitation events.
  2. Stewardship strengthens resilience. Many large natural landscapes and managed conservation lands demonstrated strong resilience across multiple hazards. Fire-adapted ecosystems and actively managed landscapes frequently exhibited higher wildfire resilience, reflecting the important role of prescribed fire, fuel management, and long-term ecological stewardship throughout central Florida.
  3. Vulnerability emerges where systems overlap. Areas exhibiting lower resilience often reflected overlapping stressors such as infrastructure sensitivity, altered hydrology, development pressure, impervious surfaces, or repeated exposure to storm impacts. In many cases, vulnerability was driven less by the presence of hazards themselves than by the interaction between hazards and landscape condition.
  4. Every parcel tells a different story. Perhaps most importantly, resilience was not evenly distributed. Adjacent parcels often exhibited substantially different resilience scores depending on hydrologic connectivity, land cover, soil characteristics, infrastructure proximity, and management history. This parcel-scale variation makes the framework especially valuable for identifying where conservation, restoration, and management actions can have the greatest impact.

These patterns clarify where landscapes are already reinforcing readiness and ecological function, and where targeted action may reduce long-term risk. (See maps)

From Maps to Decisions

Each parcel received a resilience score ranging from 1 to 5 for each hazard. Individual hazard scores were then integrated into a composite resilience framework designed to identify areas that consistently perform well across multiple resilience dimensions.

Rather than simply identifying where hazards occur, the framework identifies where landscapes are best equipped to withstand them.

The resulting maps help identify:

  • Existing resilience anchors that support long-term landscape function
  • Areas where restoration or conservation investments may improve resilience
  • Locations where vulnerabilities may warrant additional planning or management attention
  • Resilience investments aligned with military readiness and conservation objectives
Technical details:

The parcel-scale framework adapts the DoD Extreme Conditions Assessment Tool concept to Sentinel Landscape planning. For each hazard, geospatial indicators were summarized to parcels with zonal statistics and converted to standardized 1-5 scores. Hazard scores were then combined into a weighted composite index, with outputs delivered in GIS-ready and tabular formats.

Applications

The APAFRSL resilience assessment is designed to support collaborative planning across military, conservation, and regional partners. By evaluating multiple hazards within a common parcel-scale framework, the scorecard provides a consistent foundation for identifying shared priorities and coordinating investments.

Potential applications include:

  • Integration with the Central Florida Regional Planning Council's Regional Resiliency Toolkit for Inland Governments
  • Regional resilience planning efforts
  • Conservation prioritization
  • Military readiness and mission support
  • Funding and grant applications
  • Natural infrastructure investments
  • Partner coordination and decision support
Why It Matters

The resilience assessment demonstrates that many of the landscape features supporting military readiness, agricultural productivity, and ecological integrity are the same features that strengthen long-term resilience.

Wetlands, recharge areas, fire-adapted ecosystems, working lands, and connected natural landscapes provide measurable benefits across multiple hazards. By translating these benefits into parcel-scale resilience scores, the Resilience Scorecard makes resilience visible, measurable, and actionable.

The result is a practical decision-support tool that supports strategic conservation, resilience investments, and long-term landscape stewardship while strengthening the connection between military readiness, ecosystem function, and community resilience throughout Central Florida.
